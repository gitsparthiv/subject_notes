CYBER SECURITY (PECCS702E) 
Course Outcomes and Suggestions for UT-II 
Course Outcomes (COs) 
After completing this course, students will be able to: 
CO1: Explain the fundamentals of cyber security, cyberspace, the CIA triad, cyber threats, cyber warfare and 
cyber terrorism, and their implications for critical infrastructure and organizations. 
(Bloom's Level: K1-Knowledge and K2 – Understand) 
CO2: Classify types of hackers, cyber -attacks and malware, and analyze the stages of a system hack, from 
gaining access to covering tracks. 
(Bloom's Level: K4 – Analyze) 
CO3: Apply ethical hacking concepts, threat modelling, VAPT and security architecture principles, and devise 
defenses against social engineering and insider threats. 
(Bloom's Level: K3 – Apply) 
CO4: Demonstrate the cyber forensics investigation process, evidence handling and report writing, and plan 
an information security audit based on ISO 27001:2013. 
(Bloom's Level:  K6 – Create) 
CO5: Interpret the provisions of the IT Act 2000 on e -commerce, e -governance, certifying authorities, 
offences and penalties, IPR in cyberspace, and network-layer security (IPSec). 
(Bloom's Level:  K5 – Evaluate)

Section II: Short Answer Questions 
Q1. Define cyber security.  [CO1] 
Answer: Protection of systems, networks, programs and data from digital attacks, damage or 
unauthorized access. 
Q2. What is cyber terrorism?  [CO1] 
Answer: Use of computers or networks to cause fear, disruption or harm for political, ideological or 
religious goals, e.g., attacks on power grids.

Q3. Differentiate a hacker from a cracker.  [CO2] 
Answer: A hacker explores systems (possibly ethically); a cracker breaks security with malicious intent, 
e.g., to steal or damage. 
Q4. What is privilege escalation?  [CO2] 
Answer: Gaining higher access rights than originally granted: vertical (user to admin) or horizontal 
(user to another user). 
Q5. What is an insider threat?  [CO3] 
Answer: A security risk from people within the organization (employees, contractors) who misuse 
legitimate access. 
Q6. Define a vulnerability and an exploit.  [CO3] 
Answer: Vulnerability: a weakness in a system. Exploit: code or a technique that takes advantage of 
that weakness. 
Q7. What is volatile evidence? Give an example.  [CO4] 
Answer: Data lost when power is off, e.g., RAM contents, running processes, network connections. 
Q8. What is a hash value's role in forensics?  [CO4] 
Answer: It verifies that the forensic image matches the original (integrity), e.g., using MD5 or SHA-256. 
Q9. Who appoints the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA), and under which section?  [CO5] 
Answer: The Central Government, under Section 17 of the IT Act 2000. 
Q10. Name two modes of IPSec.  [CO5] 
Answer: Transport mode (protects the payload only) and tunnel mode (protects the entire IP packet; 
used in VPNs).
