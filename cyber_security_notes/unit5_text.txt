Intellectual Property Rights 
Intellectual Property (IP) is the property created by an individual or a group of individuals 
using their own intellect for ultimate use in commerce. This property is not available in the 
public domain till its official declaration and registration. Exampl es of intellectual property 
can be a symbol, a word, or a logo, etc., an invention relating to a product or any process, a 
new design, a literary or artistic work, or a trademark. 
Intellectual Property Rights  (IPR) is the statutory right granted by the gov ernment to the 
owner(s) or inventor(s) of the intellectual property. IPR includes copyright, trademarks, 
industrial design rights, patents, etc. This right is given to the application(s) to prohibit others 
from exploiting the IP commercially in lieu of the  disclosure of his/her IP in the IPR 
application for a given period of time. It allows IPR owner(s) to make a profit from their 
invented material(s). Thus it encourages creativity and innovation.

and in any manner he wants. The owner wants to stop others from manufacturing and selling

S/he may also l ike to sell or license the IP to some companies or organizations for

owner of the intellectual property after a search by the government authority dealing with 
IPR. It also shows the creators‟ competence in innovations in the field. This certificate also 
establishes the valid and legal right about the intellectual property that  the inventor(s) has 
created.

Plagiarism 
The word ‘plagiarism’ was derived from the Latin word ‘plagiare’. ‘P lagiare’ in the Latin 
language means to abduct or kidnap. Plagiarism is “the act of presenting the words, ideas, 
--- Page 4 ---
images, sounds, or the creative expression of others as it is your creation or your own” . It is 
very significant to understand the importance of plagiarism with respect to academics a nd 
creativity. Plagiarism is the stealing of intellectual property of other creators. It is also 
considered as cheating and unethical activities. Normally these illegal activities are treated 
as academic theft and thus come under academic offenses. 
There a re two types of plagiarism - intentional and unintentional. Copying others’ work, 
cut-paste from electronic resources , borrowing assignments, and d ownloading text from 
the internet, and presenting it as our own work  are examples of intentional plagiarism . If 
we do not acknowledge through proper paraphrasing, summarizing , and quotation , then 
that becomes unintentional plagiarism. C areless copying from electronic databases, quoting 
excessively, and f ailure to use our own words are also examples of unintention al 
plagiarism. 
Using our own ideas and developing our own writing skills is the best way to avoid 
plagiarism. If these are not essentially possible, we must cite the sources. That means, we 
must acknowledge the sources  when someone else's ideas are used . We have to explicitly 
tell our readers where the ideas are derived from. Rewriting others’ ideas in our own words 
can also solve the problem. While we need to write about a topic from some sources, we 
have to take careful notes from the sources and write in our own words on the topic. 
 
Digital Rights Management 
Digital rights management (DRM) is a scheme to control copyrighted material access using 
technological aids. Here, we should apply digital technologies so that copyrighted materials 
remain in the rea d-only mode and copying of the same could be restricted. DRM includes 
some computer instructions (or codes) that restrict copying or limit the number of accessing 
digital products or services. Encryption-decryption schemes may also be used to allow access 
of copyrighted materials like software, e -books, data, digital content, etc. to the intended 
licensed users. The license should be provided with encryption keys and users who have 
decryption keys will be able to access the digital materials. 
There are many  ways to protect our digital materials such as software, content, and other 
services. We may restrict a user from saving, editing, sharing the digital materials. We may 
also restrict the printing of some artworks or documents for a limited number of times.  It is 
also possible to disable or restrict the screenshot capture facilities of the computer. If we set 
an expiry date of our digital products and services, then these will not be opened or accessible 
after that fixed time. Such setting of the expiry date  for the digital materials will be another 
option for DRM. These restrictions may be imposed based on the IP address or MAC address 
of our digital devices. Incorporating watermark artworks is another way to establish 
ownership and identity of the innovation or creator of the digital products and services. 
--- Page 5 ---
DRM is facing great challenges nowadays. It is a common fact that everybody is not in 
agreement upon digital rights management. For example, when we pay for music on a 
specific device or app, we may wish to be able to play it on any other devices or apps that we 
love. But often DRM does not permit such freedom. 
There are a number of benefits of DRM. DRM ensures ownership of the authors of their 
digital products and services in addition to a guarantee of inc ome streams from their creative 
works. These will motivate others to be creative or innovative that will definitely elevate 
human civilization through technological progress. DRM also assists us to secure our digital 
repositories and keep them private and safe. It offers better ways for licensing agreements 
and technological distributions. It also teaches digital practitioners about IPR and copyright 
issues. 
 
 
Licensing: Creative Commons, GPL and Apache 
A license is a legal document that provides binding (or agreement) guidelines  to the 
individual(s) or organization(s) as per law regarding the distribution of some products or 
services. Software is also a product that offers various services and hence, comes under the 
umbrella of licensing for its distribut ion. Software licensing provides the right to make 
multiple copies of the software without violating copyrights to the end -users. It also 
characterizes the responsibilities of the parties entering into the license agreement. It may 
enforce restrictions on how the software will be used. The restrictions include terms and 
conditions on fair use, protections, limitations, warranties, and disclaimers. 
There are many benefits of software licensing. The right software license can save our money 
by using unlicense d software against the law. We can avail of round -the-clock maintenance 
support against the license for which we have paid. We can claim damages according to the 
agreement in case any such event arises. The license offers a feel -good and confidence while 
having such copy compared to the situation of using pirated software copies.  
There are three systems of software licensing platforms mostly used in the present time, 
namely, Creative Commons (CC), General Public License (GPL), and Apache. These are 
presented briefly here. 
 
Creative Commons (CC):  It is an internationally active non -profit organization. This 
organization provides free licenses for the inventors or creators to make use of it when 
making their work available to the public in advance under certa in conditions. When an 
article is written or a picture is captured, these digital documents or materials are 
automatically protected by copyright. Such protection restricts others from using these 
materials in their own way like putting them online. The cr eator can select how they want 
others to use the work under this license agreement. Users know what they can and can‟t do 
when the work creator publishes his/her work under CC license. Users can use the work if 
s/he gets permission from the creator only. Interestingly, CC licenses enable works to be used 
for education purposes by anyone or an organization without financial profits. Hence, 
teachers and students can freely copy, share and sometimes modify a CC work without 
having the permission of the creator.

Advantages of CC license include creators‟ work will be freely available, shareable, and 
usable online as per permissions applied on creative work. If permission is given, then 
additional improvement in creative work including open -source code is possibl e. CC 
licensing regime also ensures protection of the original copyright of the creator who can 
modify it within the clauses of the license. 
One of the disadvantages of a CC license is we cannot revoke a CC license once given. 
Subsequent usages can be moni tored later only in this regime. We can‟t ask for a license fee 
or compensation if one makes a profit from our work provided we have not provided a non -
commercial license. There is an ambiguity in the copyright of derived works and hence, we 
must be careful before attaching a CC license to our work. 
 
General Public License (GNU -GPL): It is the most commonly used free software license. 
GPL was introduced by Richard Stallman in 1989 of the Free Software Foundation for GNU 
(internationally acclaimed open -source OS Linux user groups) project. GPL license 
agreement permits software to be freely used, modified, and redistributed by anyone. 
WordPress is an example of software released under the GPL license. Anyone can download, 
run, modify, redistribute free copies , and distribute modified versions. GPL is based on 
copyleft (utilizing copyright law for the right to copy, share, modify and improve creative 
works of authorship) concept which plays on the word copyright. GPL itself is under the 
copyright ownership of Stallman's GNU funded „Free Software Foundation‟. 
There are many advantages of publishing software under GPL. The development of software 
in new areas is possible by regular feedback from the users. Such open source initiatives help 
the free development of several other open-source software. The developer‟s community can 
volunteer technical support for these open -source software and thus the cost of software 
maintenance will be reduced. Software bugs can easily be fixed since the number of people 
working on them is increasing. Developers get attracted to these open source GPL licensed 
software as they can try and use it without spending much. 
The major disadvantage of a GPL license is that a commercial product has to be released as 
open-source if it incorpora tes GPL licensed products. Thus, if any project uses a GPL 
licensed component, the whole project has been incorporated in the GPL banner. Because of 
this reason, major companies set a ban on the use of GPL products. Besides this, many people 
are not aware of the stringent terms of GPL. 
 
Apache License: Apache Software Foundation (ASF) of Apache Group has introduced the 
Apache license as a free and open-source software (FOSS) licensing agreement in 1995. This 
license was initially the same as the old BSD (Be rkeley Software Distribution) covered 
software license. Apache License v2 was a little more radical departure from the initial 
version of the license. 
Apache license allows copy, modification, and distribution of the covered software  in 
source or binary fo rms. It has the provision of exercising the patent right. All modified or 
unmodified copies are accompanied by a copy of the license. All modifications are to be 
clearly marked as the work of the modifier. All notices of copyright, trademark, and patent 
rights are to be reproduced accurately in distributed copies under this license.

IT Act-2000 
Information Technology Act, 2000 or IT Act-2000 is the act of the Indian Parliament notified 
on 17 th October, 2000 to provide a legal framework for electronic governance . It provides 
recognition to electronic records, digital signatures, electronic exchange of data, and 
electronic commerce transactions . It is the primary law dealing with electronic commerce 
and cybercrimes in India. The original act contained 94 sections, divided into 4 schedules and 
19 chapters. This law is inherited from the United Nations Commission for International 
Trade related Laws (UNCITRAL) model law. The laws, under this act, are applicable to the 
whole of India. It is applicable to persons of ot her nationalities if the crime involves a 
computer or network located in India.

E-Commerce  
E-Commerce or Electronic Commerce means buying and selling of goods, products, or services 
over the internet. E -commerce is also known as electronic commerce or internet commerce. 
These services provided online over the internet network. Transaction of money, funds, and data 
are also considered as E -commerce. These business transac tions can be done in four ways: 
Business to Business (B2B), Business to Customer (B2C), Customer to Customer (C2C), 
Customer to Business (C2B). The standard definition of E -commerce is a  commercial 
transaction which is happened over the internet. Online st ores like Amazon, Flipkart, Shopify, 
Myntra, Ebay, Quikr, Olx are examples of E -commerce websites. By 2020, global retail e -
commerce can reach up to $27 Trillion. Let us learn in detail about what is the advantages and 
disadvantages of E-commerce and its types. 
E-Commerce or Electronic Commerce 
E-commerce is a popular term for electronic commerce or even internet commerce. The name is 
self-explanatory, it is the meeting of buyers and sellers on the internet. This involves the 
transaction of goods and services, the transfer of funds and the exchange of data. 
So when you log into your Amazon and  purchase a book, this is a classic example of an e -
commerce transaction. Here you interact with the seller ( Amazon), exchange data in form of 
pictures, text, address for delivery etc. and then you make the payment. 
As of now, e-commerce is one of the fastest growing industries in the global economy. As per 
one estimate, it grows nearly 23% every year. And it is projected to be a $27 trillion industry by 
the end of this decade.

Types of E-Commerce Models 
Electronic commerce can be classified into four main categories. The basis for this simple 
classification is the parties that are involved in the transactions. So the four basic electronic 
commerce models are as follows, 
1. Business to Business 
This is Business to Business transactions. Here the companies are doing business with each 
other. The final  consumer is not involved. So the online transactions only involve 
the manufacturers, wholesalers, retailers etc. 
2. Business to Consumer 
Business to Consumer. Here the company will sell their goods and/or services directly to the 
consumer. The consumer can browse their websites and look at products, pictures, read reviews. 
Then they place their order and the company ships the goods directly to them. Popular examples 
are Amazon, Flipkart, Jabong etc. 
3. Consumer to Consumer 
Consumer to consumer, where the consumers are in direct contact with each other. No company 
is involved. It helps people sell their personal goods and assets directly to an interested party. 
Usually, goods traded are cars, bikes, electronics etc. OLX, Quikr etc follow this model. 
4. Consumer to Business 
This is the reverse of B2C, it is a consumer to business. So the consumer pro vides a good or 
some service to the  company. Say for example an  IT freelancer who demos and sells his 
software to a company. This would be a C2B transaction.

IP security (IPSec) 
IP Sec (Internet Protocol Security) is an Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) standard 
suite of protocols between two communication points across the IP network that provide data 
authentication, integrity, and confidentiality. It also defines the encrypted, decrypted, and 
authenticated packets. The protocols needed for secure key exchange and key management 
are defined in it. 
Uses of IP Security 
IPsec can be used to do the following things: 
 To encrypt application layer data. 
 To provide security for routers sending routing data across the public internet. 
 To provide authentication without encryption , like to authenticate that the data 
originates from a known sender.

 To protect network data by setting up circuits using IPsec tunneling in which all 
data being sent between the two endpoints is encrypted, as with a  Virtual Private 
Network(VPN) connection. 
 
Components of IP Security 
It has the following components: 
1. Encapsulating Security Payload (ESP) 
2. Authentication Header (AH) 
3. Internet Key Exchange (IKE) 
1. Encapsulatin g Security Payload (ESP):  It provides data integrity, encryption, 
authentication, and anti-replay. It also provides authentication for payload.

How does IPsec work? 
IPsec connections include the following steps: 
Key exchange: Keys are necessary for encryption; a key is a string of random characters that 
can be used to "lock" (encrypt) and "unlock" (decrypt) messages. IPsec set s up keys with a 
key exchange between the connected devices, so that each device can decrypt the other 
device's messages. 
Packet headers and trailers:  All data that is sent over a network is broken down into 
smaller pieces called packets. Packets contain b oth a payload, or the actual data being sent, 
and headers, or information about that data so that computers receiving the packets know 
what to do with them. IPsec adds several headers to data packets containing authentication 
and encryption information. IP sec also adds trailers, which go after each packet's payload 
instead of before. 
Authentication: IPsec provides authentication for each packet, like a stamp of authenticity 
on a collectible item. This ensures that packets are from a trusted source and not an attacker. 
Encryption: IPsec encrypts the payloads within each packet and each packet's IP header 
(unless transport mode is used instead of tunnel mode ). This keeps data sent over IPsec 
secure and private. 
Transmission: Encrypted IPsec packets  travel across one or more networks to their 
destination using a transport protocol. At this stage, IPsec traffic differs from regular IP 
traffic in that it most often uses  UDP as its transport protocol, rather than  TCP. TCP, the 
Transmission Control Protocol, sets up dedicated connections between devices and ensures 
that all packets arrive. UDP, the User Datagram Protocol, does not set up these dedicated 
connections. IPsec uses UDP because this allows IPsec packets to get through firewalls. 
Decryption: At the oth er end of the communication, the packets are decrypted, and 
applications (e.g. a browser) can now use the delivered data.

Features of IPSec 
1. Authentication: IPSec provides authentication of IP packets using digital 
signatures or shared secrets. This helps ensure that the packets are not tampered 
with or forged. 
2. Confidentiality: IPSec provides confidentiality by encrypting IP packets, 
preventing eavesdropping on the network traffic. 
3. Integrity: IPSec provides integrity by ensuring that IP packets have not been 
modified or corrupted during transmission. 
4. Key management: IPSec provides key management services, including key 
exchange and key revocation, to ensure that cry ptographic keys are securely 
managed. 
5. Tunneling: IPSec supports tunneling, allowing IP packets to be encapsulated 
within another protocol, such as GRE (Generic Routing Encapsulation) or L2TP 
(Layer 2 Tunneling Protocol). 
6. Flexibility: IPSec can be configure d to provide security for a wide range of 
network topologies, including point -to-point, site -to-site, and remote access 
connections. 
7. Interoperability: IPSec is an open standard protocol, which means that it is 
supported by a wide range of vendors and can b e used in heterogeneous 
environments. 
 
Advantages of IPSec 
1. Strong security: IPSec provides strong cryptographic security services that help 
protect sensitive data and ensure network privacy and integrity. 
2. Wide compatibility: IPSec is an open standard proto col that is widely supported 
by vendors and can be used in heterogeneous environments. 
3. Flexibility: IPSec can be configured to provide security for a wide range of 
network topologies, including point -to-point, site -to-site, and remote access 
connections. 
4. Scalability: IPSec can be used to secure large -scale networks and can be scaled 
up or down as needed. 
5. Improved network performance: IPSec can help improve network performance 
by reducing network congestion and improving network efficiency. 
 
Disadvantages of IPSec 
1. Configuration complexity: IPSec can be complex to configure and requires 
specialized knowledge and skills. 
2. Compatibility issues: IPSec can have compatibility issues with some network 
devices and applications, which can lead to interoperability problems. 
3. Performance impact: IPSec can impact network performance due to the 
overhead of encryption and decryption of IP packets. 
4. Key management: IPSec requires effective key management to ensure the 
security of the cryptographic keys used for encryption and authentication. 
5. Limited protection: IPSec only provides protection for IP traffic, and other 
protocols such as ICMP, DNS, and routing protocols may still be vulnerable to 
attacks.
